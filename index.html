Why your laptop isn’t enough

You forecast storms. A 10-day forecast on a coarse map takes your laptop about an hour. You want a sharper map, and you want it by morning.

A laptop that lives in the data center

Illinois Computes Research Notebooks (ICRN) gives you Jupyter, RStudio or VS Code in your browser. Nothing to install, and your work keeps running when you close the lid. It is still one seat: about the speed of one laptop core.

The cost of communication

The map is split into patches, one per core (domain decomposition). Each step, neighbors swap edge values over MPI: a halo exchange.

Where your job lands matters

Cores on the same node share memory, so they talk almost for free. Cores on different nodes talk over the network, which every job on the cluster shares. Slurm picks your nodes.

Try: switch the placement

Cluster

You don’t press Run. You submit.

On a cluster you write down what you need, hand it to the scheduler, and walk away. It runs your job when enough nodes are free.
